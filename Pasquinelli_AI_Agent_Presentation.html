Architecture

Three layers make this agent work: Corpus · Model · Agent Loop

LAYER 1

The Corpus

What it reads

• All Pasquinelli lab papers (2000–2025): ~55 papers

• Field-defining papers: Bartel, Ambros, Tuschl labs

• PubMed abstracts, PMC full text, GEO metadata

• Lab protocols, grant aims, meeting notes (optional)

LAYER 2

The Model

How it learns

• Base: Llama 3, Mistral, or GPT-4 via API

• Method: RAG or supervised fine-tuning on corpus

• Hardware: RTX 3050 Ti (already in the lab)

• Time to first version: 1–2 days setup + overnight

LAYER 3

The Agent Loop

How it acts

• Reads program.md (your research strategy)

• Iterates: reads → extracts → hypothesizes → ranks

• Outputs: tables, hypothesis boards, reading queues

• Human stays in control: review, redirect, refine

Example Prompts — Amy Pasquinelli (PI)

Strategic questions that used to take days to answer

Grant strategy

"Summarize the strongest open questions in miRNA-mediated aging regulation in C. elegans after 2020. Rank them by how well-supported they are and how testable they are with our current tools."

→ Ranked hypothesis table with citations, evidence strength scores, and suggested experimental approaches.

Mechanism mapping

"Map the evidence for how ALG-1 and ALG-2 produce opposing aging phenotypes. What are the proposed mechanisms? What is still unresolved?"

→ Mechanistic diagram in text form, evidence table, list of unresolved questions with citations.

Cross-paper synthesis

"Compare our heat-shock recovery paper (2021) with our aging RNA-seq paper (2024). Which miRNAs appear in both contexts? What does that suggest about shared regulatory programs?"

→ Overlap analysis, shared gene/miRNA lists, synthesis paragraph with citations.

Field positioning

"How does our lab's expression-not-sequence interpretation of miRNA target specificity compare to the current consensus? Who agrees, who disagrees, and what would resolve it?"

→ Field map with lab positions, supporting/contradicting papers, resolution experiments.

Example Prompts — Postdocs & Graduate Students

Daily research acceleration for every lab member

Experimental design

"I'm planning a follow-up to the miR-238/239ab longevity paper. What are the three most testable hypotheses that would extend this work? What controls would be critical for each?"

→ Three hypothesis cards with experimental design, controls, and relevant citations.

Literature catch-up

"I just joined the lab. Give me a structured reading path from the foundational let-7 papers through the most recent aging work. Start with what I need to understand the 2024 NAR paper."

→ Tiered reading list with annotations, estimated reading time, key concepts per paper.

Data interpretation

"We found that transcript X has a short poly(A) tail but high translation efficiency. Is this an outlier? What mechanisms could explain it based on the PABP literature?"

→ Literature-grounded interpretation, candidate mechanisms, papers to read.

Manuscript prep

"Check the consistency between our methods section and figure legends for the heat-shock recovery paper. Flag any discrepancies in sample sizes, strain names, or statistical tests."

→ Discrepancy table with line numbers and suggested corrections.

Journal club prep

"Summarize this new paper on miRNA-mediated translational repression in 5 points. How does it relate to our lab's work? What questions should we ask the authors?"

→ Summary, relevance assessment, 3–5 discussion questions.

Overnight Autonomous Research

Let the agent work while you sleep — wake up to structured results

Task 1: Literature sweep

"Search for all papers published in the last 6 months on miRNA regulation in aging organisms. Summarize each, score relevance to our lab's work (1–10), and flag any that contradict our published findings."

→ Ranked paper list · Relevance scores · Contradiction flags

Task 2: Cross-dataset replication

"Download all public C. elegans aging RNA-seq datasets from GEO. Score each using the gene signatures from our 2024 NAR paper. Report which signatures replicate and which do not."

→ Replication score table · Forest plots · Core aging marker gene set

Task 3: Target concordance

"For let-7 and miR-238, pull predictions from TargetScan and miRDB. Cross-reference against our published functional data. Build a concordance score for each predicted target."

→ Concordance-scored target table · Venn diagrams · High-confidence target shortlist

Task 4: Hypothesis generation

"Starting from our 2021 heat-shock recovery paper, find all related papers on miRNA-mediated stress recovery. Generate 10 testable hypotheses ranked by evidence strength and experimental feasibility."

→ Ranked hypothesis board · Citations · Experimental sketches

The agent runs all four tasks in parallel overnight. By morning you have a structured research briefing, not a search results page.

Technical Approach

Two paths to a lab-specific LLM — choose based on your goals

DimensionRAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation)Fine-Tuning
Setup timeHours to daysDays to weeks
Hardware neededCPU or small GPUGPU (RTX 3050 Ti sufficient)
Corpus updateAdd files, re-index (minutes)Retrain (hours)
Answers cite sourcesYes, automaticallyRequires prompting
Best forLiterature Q&A, hypothesis generationStyle adaptation, specialized reasoning
CostNear-zero (local) or low API costNear-zero (local)
RecommendedSTART HERE →Add later for specialized tasks

RAG in plain English: The model looks up relevant passages from your corpus before answering — like giving the LLM a searchable library of your papers.

Fine-tuning in plain English: You train the model's weights on your corpus so the knowledge is baked in. More powerful but more effort to update.
